## Population in Statistics

**Definition:** Population is the complete group of people, objects, or items that we want to study or collect information about.

**Example:** If a school has 1,000 students and we want to find their average marks, all 1,000 students are called the population.

**In Simple Words:** Population means the whole group that we want to study.

**Remember:**
- **Population:** The entire group.
- **Sample:** A small part of the population selected for study.



 **Sample in Statistics:**
**Definition:** A sample is a small part or group selected from the whole population to collect information and study it.

**Example:** If a school has 1,000 students and we select only 100 students to find their average marks, those 100 students are called the sample.

**In Simple Words:** A sample is a small part of the whole population selected for study.

**Remember:**
- Population: The entire group.
- Sample: A small part of the entire group.



## Classification of Population in Statistics

Population is mainly classified into four types:

### 1. Finite Population
A population that has a limited or countable number of items is called a finite population.

**Example:** Total students in a school (1,000 students).

### 2. Infinite Population
A population that has an unlimited number of items or observations is called an infinite population.

**Example:** Possible outcomes of tossing a coin repeatedly without end.

### 3. Existing Population
A population that consists of people, objects, or things that actually exist is called an existing population.

**Example:** All students in a college.

### 4. Hypothetical Population
A population that consists of all possible outcomes of an experiment or process is called a hypothetical population.

**Example:** All possible outcomes of repeatedly tossing a coin.

### Summary

| Type | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Finite Population | Limited number of items |
| Infinite Population | Unlimited number of items |
| Existing Population | Things that actually exist |
| Hypothetical Population | All possible outcomes of an experiment |

**Note:** Population can be classified in different ways depending on the statistics textbook.


## Census Survey in Statistics

### Definition
A **Census Survey** is a method of collecting information from every member of the entire population. In this method, no one is left out.

### Example
Suppose a school has 1,000 students and the teacher collects information from all 1,000 students.

This is called a **Census Survey** because information is collected from the whole population.

### Need / Importance of Census Survey

1. **Complete Information:** It provides information about every member of the population.
2. **Accuracy:** It gives detailed information about the entire population.
3. **Government Planning:** It helps the government make plans for education, healthcare, housing, etc.
4. **No Sampling Error:** Since the whole population is studied, there is no sampling error.
5. **Detailed Study:** It helps us understand the characteristics of the entire population.

### Advantages of Census Survey

- Covers the entire population.
- Provides detailed information.
- Useful for future planning and decision-making.
- No sampling error.

### Disadvantages of Census Survey

- Takes more time.
- Requires more money.
- Needs more workers.
- Difficult to conduct for very large populations.

### In Simple Words
A Census Survey means **collecting information from every person or item in the whole population instead of selecting only a few people.**

**Example:** India's Population Census collects information about people across the country.


## Sample Survey in Statistics

### Definition
A **Sample Survey** is a method of collecting information from a small part (sample) of the entire population instead of collecting information from everyone.

### Example
Suppose a school has 1,000 students, and the teacher selects only 100 students to collect information about their average marks.

This is called a **Sample Survey** because information is collected from only a part of the population.

### Need / Importance of Sample Survey

1. **Saves Time:** It takes less time to collect information.
2. **Less Expensive:** It requires less money than a census survey.
3. **Easy to Manage:** It is easier to collect and handle data from a small group.
4. **Quick Results:** Results can be obtained quickly.
5. **Useful for Large Populations:** It is helpful when studying the entire population is difficult.

### Advantages of Sample Survey

- Saves time and money.
- Requires fewer workers.
- Easy to conduct.
- Gives quick results.
- Useful for large populations.

### Disadvantages of Sample Survey

- Results may not be fully accurate.
- Sampling errors may occur.
- The sample may not represent the whole population.
- Wrong sample selection can give misleading results.

### In Simple Words
A Sample Survey means **collecting information from a small group of people instead of the entire population.**

### Difference Between Census Survey and Sample Survey

| Census Survey | Sample Survey |
|---|---|
| Studies the entire population. | Studies only a part of the population. |
| Takes more time. | Takes less time. |
| More expensive. | Less expensive. |
| No sampling error. | Sampling error may occur. |
| Requires more workers. | Requires fewer workers. |

**Example:** Studying all 1,000 students is a Census Survey, while studying only 100 students is a Sample Survey.


## Sampling Error in Statistics

### Definition
**Sampling Error** is the difference between the result obtained from a sample and the actual result of the entire population.

It happens because we study only a small part of the population instead of studying everyone.

### Example
Suppose a school has 1,000 students, and we select 100 students to find their average marks.

- Average marks of all 1,000 students = 75
- Average marks of 100 selected students = 70

**Sampling Error = 75 - 70 = 5**

The difference of 5 is called the Sampling Error.

### Causes of Sampling Error

1. **Small Sample Size:** Selecting too few people may not represent the whole population.
2. **Random Variation:** Different samples may give different results.
3. **Biased Selection:** Selecting people unfairly may give incorrect results.
4. **Unrepresentative Sample:** The selected sample may not represent the whole population.

### Solutions / Methods to Reduce Sampling Error

1. **Increase Sample Size:** Select more people to get better results.
2. **Random Sampling:** Select people


## Non-Sampling Error in Statistics

### Definition
**Non-Sampling Error** is an error that occurs during the collection, recording, processing, or analysis of data due to mistakes or other problems.

It can occur in both Census Surveys and Sample Surveys.

### Example
Suppose a teacher collects marks from 100 students, but accidentally records 75 as 57.

This mistake is called a **Non-Sampling Error**.

### Causes of Non-Sampling Error

1. **Wrong Information:** Respondents provide incorrect information.
2. **Recording Mistakes:** Data is recorded incorrectly.
3. **Questionnaire Errors:** Questions are unclear or confusing.
4. **Non-Response:** Some people do not provide the required information.
5. **Data Processing Errors:** Mistakes happen while calculating or processing data.

### Solutions / Methods to Reduce Non-Sampling Error

1. **Ask Clear Questions:** Use simple and understandable questions.
2. **Proper Training:** Train people who collect the data.
3. **Check Data:** Verify information before recording it.
4. **Avoid Mistakes:** Carefully enter and process data.
5. **Follow Up:** Contact people who have not provided the required information.

### In Simple Words
Non-Sampling Error means **mistakes that happen while collecting, recording, or handling data, rather than errors caused by selecting a sample.**

### Conclusion
Non-Sampling Errors can be reduced by collecting correct information, checking data carefully, and using proper methods.

### Difference Between Sampling Error and Non-Sampling Error

| Sampling Error | Non-Sampling Error |
|---|---|
| Occurs due to selecting a sample instead of the whole population. | Occurs due to mistakes in collecting or handling data. |
| Occurs in Sample Surveys. | Occurs in both Census and Sample Surveys. |
| Can be reduced by increasing sample size and proper sampling. | Can be reduced by careful data collection and checking. |
| Related to sample selection. | Related to data collection and processing. |


## Sampling Design in Statistics

### Definition
**Sampling Design** is a plan or method used to select a sample from the entire population for a survey or study.

It explains how, from whom, and how many people or items will be selected for research.

### Example
Suppose a school has 1,000 students, and we want to collect information about their study habits.

We decide to select 100 students randomly from the school.

This plan of selecting 100 students is called **Sampling Design**.

### Objectives of Sampling Design

1. **Accuracy:** To get reliable and accurate results.
2. **Save Time:** To collect information in less time.
3. **Reduce Cost:** To complete the survey with less money.
4. **Proper Representation:** To select a sample that represents the whole population.
5. **Reduce Bias:** To make the selection process fair.

### Types of Sampling Design

Sampling Design is mainly divided into two types:

#### 1. Probability Sampling
In this method, every member of the population has a known chance of being selected.

**Examples:**
- Simple Random Sampling
- Systematic Sampling
- Stratified Sampling
- Cluster Sampling

#### 2. Non-Probability Sampling
In this method, every member of the population does not have a known or equal chance of being selected.

**Examples:**
- Convenience Sampling
- Judgment Sampling
- Quota Sampling
- Snowball Sampling

#### 3. Mixed Sampling

In this method, two or more sampling methods are combined to select a sample.

**Examples:**

Stratified + Simple Random Sampling

Cluster + Convenience Sampling

Multistage sampling using different methods at different stages
### In Simple Words
Sampling Design means **deciding how to select a small group (sample) from a large group (population) for a study.**

### Conclusion
A good sampling design helps us select a suitable sample, save time and money, and obtain reliable results.

## Types of Non-Probability Sampling

In Non-Probability Sampling, members of the population are selected without random selection. Their chances of being selected are not known.

### 1. Convenience Sampling

In this method, samples are selected based on their easy availability.

**Example:** Asking students who are easily available in a classroom to participate in a survey.

### 2. Judgment (Purposive) Sampling

In this method, the researcher selects people based on their own knowledge or judgment.

**Example:** Selecting experienced teachers to collect information about the education system.

### 3. Quota Sampling

In this method, the population is divided into different groups, and a fixed number of people is selected from each group without random selection.
**Type**
1.Controlled 
2.Uncontrolled

**Example:** Selecting 20 boys and 20 girls for a survey.

### 4. Snowball Sampling

In this method, existing participants help the researcher find more participants by referring other people.

**Example:** Asking one person to recommend other people who have experience with a particular subject.

### In Simple Words

| Type                 | Meaning                                         |
| -------------------- | ----------------------------------------------- |
| Convenience Sampling | Selecting easily available people               |
| Judgment Sampling    | Selecting people based on researcher's judgment |
| Quota Sampling       | Selecting a fixed number from different groups  |
| Snowball Sampling    | Existing participants refer new participants    |


# Types of Snowball Sampling in Statistics

## Definition
**Snowball Sampling** is a type of Non-Probability Sampling in which existing participants help the researcher find new participants through referrals.

The sample grows gradually, like a snowball.

## Types of Snowball Sampling

### 1. Linear Snowball Sampling

**Definition:** In Linear Snowball Sampling, each participant refers only one new participant to the researcher.

**Example:**
- Person A refers Person B.
- Person B refers Person C.
- Person C refers Person D.
- Person D refers Person E.

**Pattern:**
```text
A → B → C → D → E
```

**In Simple Words:** One participant refers only one new participant, creating a single chain.

---

### 2. Exponential Non-Discriminative Snowball Sampling

**Definition:** In this method, each participant refers multiple new participants, and all eligible referred participants are included in the study.

**Example:**
- 2 initial participants refer 4 people.
- Those 4 people refer 8 people.
- Those 8 people refer 16 people.

**Pattern:**
```text
       2
       ↓
       4
       ↓
       8
       ↓
      16
```

**In Simple Words:** Each participant refers multiple people, and all eligible referrals are included in the survey.

---

### 3. Exponential Discriminative Snowball Sampling

**Definition:** In this method, each participant refers multiple new participants, but the researcher selects only those who meet specific requirements or criteria.

**Example:**
- 2 initial participants refer 4 people.
- The researcher selects only 2 suitable people.
- Those 2 selected people refer 4 more people.
- Again, the researcher selects suitable participants.

**Pattern:**
```text
2 Initial Participants
          ↓
    4 Referrals
          ↓
  2 Selected People
          ↓
    4 New Referrals
          ↓
  Further Selection
```

**In Simple Words:** Each participant refers multiple people, but the researcher selects only suitable participants for the study.

---

## Difference Between Types of Snowball Sampling

| Type | Selection Method |
|---|---|
| Linear Snowball Sampling | One participant refers one new participant. |
| Exponential Non-Discriminative | Multiple referrals are made and all eligible referrals are included. |
| Exponential Discriminative | Multiple referrals are made, but only suitable participants are selected. |

## Advantages of Snowball Sampling

1. Useful for finding hard-to-reach people.
2. Saves time and money.
3. Helps researchers find more participants.
4. Useful when a complete list of the population is unavailable.

## Disadvantages of Snowball Sampling

1. May create selection bias.
2. Participants may refer people from similar social groups.
3. The sample may not represent the entire population.
4. Results may not be suitable for generalizing to everyone.

## Conclusion

Snowball Sampling is useful when researchers cannot easily find participants. Existing participants help identify new participants through referrals.

**Remember:** All three methods are types of Non-Probability Sampling.


# Limitations of Non-Probability Sampling

### Definition
Non-Probability Sampling is a method of selecting a sample in which people are not selected randomly. The researcher selects participants according to their convenience, choice, or judgment.

### Limitations of Non-Probability Sampling

**1. Selection Bias**

The researcher may select people according to their own choice or convenience. This can make the results unfair.

**Example:** A researcher asks only their friends about a product. Their opinions may not represent everyone's opinions.

**2. Not Representative of the Population**

The selected sample may not represent the whole population because some groups may be left out.

**Example:** If a survey is conducted only among college students, it cannot represent the opinions of all people.

**3. Less Accuracy**

The results may not be fully accurate because the selected people may have different opinions from the rest of the population.

**Example:** Asking only 10 students about the education system may not give the correct opinion of 1,000 students.

**4. Difficult to Generalize Results**

The results obtained from the sample cannot always be applied to the entire population.

**Example:** If 20 people in one city like a product, it does not mean that everyone in the country will like it.

**5. Personal Judgment**

Sometimes, the researcher selects participants based on personal choice or thinking. This may affect the final results.

**Example:** A researcher selects only intelligent students for a study about school performance.

**6. Unequal Chance of Selection**

Every person does not have an equal or known chance of being selected. Some people may get more chances than others.

**Example:** A researcher asks only people who are available in a shopping mall instead of people from different areas.

**7. Difficult to Measure Sampling Error**

In Non-Probability Sampling, the chance of selecting each person is not known. Therefore, the sampling error cannot usually be calculated using standard probability sampling formulas.

**8. Possibility of Wrong Results**

If the selected sample does not represent the population, the research results may be misleading.

**Example:** A researcher asks only cricket fans about the most popular sport. The result may show cricket as the most popular sport, even though other people may prefer different sports.

**9. Limited Reliability**

If another researcher selects a different group of people, the results may be different. This makes the findings less consistent.

**10. Difficult to Compare Results**

Different researchers may use different methods to select participants. Therefore, comparing the results of different studies can become difficult.

### In Simple Words

Non-Probability Sampling has some limitations because people are not selected randomly. The sample may be biased, may not represent the whole population, and may give results that are not fully accurate.

### Conclusion

Non-Probability Sampling is easy, quick, and less expensive, but it has limitations such as selection bias, less accuracy, and difficulty in applying the results to the entire population.

## Parameter in Statistics

### Definition
A **Parameter** is a numerical value that describes a characteristic of the entire population.

### Example
Suppose a school has 1,000 students, and the average marks of all students are 75.

Here, **75 is the Parameter** because it represents the average marks of the entire population.

### Types of Parameters
1. **Population Mean (μ):** Average value of the population.
2. **Population Proportion (P):** Percentage of the population having a particular characteristic.
3. **Population Variance (σ²):** Measures how much population values differ from the mean.
4. **Population Standard Deviation (σ):** Measures how spread out the population values are.

### Difference Between Parameter and Statistic

| Parameter | Statistic |
|---|---|
| Describes the entire population. | Describes a sample. |
| Based on the whole population. | Based on a sample. |
| Example: Population Mean (μ). | Example: Sample Mean (x̄). |

### In Simple Words
A **Parameter** is a numerical value that tells us something about the whole population.

## Statistic in Sampling Distribution

### Definition

A **statistic** is a number calculated from a sample to describe the sample or give information about the whole population.

### Explanation

When we select a sample from a population, we calculate values such as the average, proportion or variation. These calculated values are called statistics.

If we select different samples from the same population, the statistic may have different values because each sample may contain different observations.

The distribution of these statistic values from all possible samples of the same size is called the **sampling distribution**.

### Examples of Statistics

- **Sample Mean:** Average of the sample.
- **Sample Proportion:** Shows the part of a sample with a particular characteristic.
- **Sample Variance:** Shows how much the sample values differ from each other.
- **Sample Standard Deviation:** Shows how spread out the sample values are.

### Important Points

- A statistic is calculated from sample data.
- It describes a sample.
- Its value can change from sample to sample.
- It helps us understand or estimate the population.
